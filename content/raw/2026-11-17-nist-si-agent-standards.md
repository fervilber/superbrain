# NIST — Super Intelligence (SI) Agent Standards Initiative (capturado 2026-11-17)

Categoría: Estándares, identidad y seguridad de agentes. Fuente primaria: hub oficial del NIST. Cobertura secundaria: aiagentthreats.com («NIST AI Agent Standards Initiative: What Exists in 2026»), montrealethics.ai (AI Policy Corner, 10-nov-2026), NIST SP 2400-102e2026 (9-nov-2026), y el anuncio de Microsoft del 17-nov-2026.

## Extractos verbatim — hub oficial del NIST

> The Super Intelligence (SI) Agent Standards Initiative ensures that the next generation of SI—agents capable of autonomous actions—is widely adopted with confidence. By fostering industry-led technical standards and open protocols, NIST aims to catalyze an ecosystem where agents function securely on behalf of users and interoperate smoothly across the digital landscape while cementing U.S. dominance at the technological frontier.

> ### Strategic Pillars
>
> 1. **Facilitating Industry-led Standards.** NIST hosts technical convenings and conducts gap analyses to produce voluntary guidelines to inform industry-led standardization for SI agents. NIST collaborates with the interagency, including NSF, to expand stakeholder engagement in and maintain leadership on SI agents at international standards bodies.
> 2. **Fostering Community-led Protocols.** NIST engages with the SI ecosystem to identify and reduce barriers to interoperable agent protocols. NSF invests in the development and security of open-source ecosystems including SI agent protocol ecosystems via its Pathways to Enable Secure Open-Source Ecosystems program.
> 3. **Investing in Research.** NIST conducts fundamental research into agent authentication and identity infrastructure to enable secure human-agent and multi-agent interactions. NIST develops state-of-the-art security evaluations to inform protocol development and consumer comparison.

## Extractos verbatim — estado real del programa (aiagentthreats.com, 1 de noviembre de 2026)

> As of 1 November 2026, **NIST has not published an AI agent standard.** The NIST AI Agent Standards Initiative page still lists only a request for information, a concept paper and listening sessions. NIST's newest agent item is a 27 October blog post, "Design Tenets for Agent Identity Management Systems", which sets out ten identity tenets for agents.

> The authors write that "we have proposed an initial set of ten design tenets". It is a blog post, not a formal NIST publication: it is not a Special Publication, not a standard, and it states no comment period. The tenets are: **1. Distinct Agent Identification · 2. Agent Linking, Delegation & Attenuation · 3. Least & Zero Standing Privilege · 4. Short-Lived, Dynamic & Revocable Authorization · 5. Human-in-the-Loop Triggers · 6. Mandates and Constraints · 7. Architectural Separation of Policy from Reasoning · 8. Deterministic Access Decisions · 9. Continuous Monitoring · 10. Immutable Evidence.**

> ### What is still missing
>
> - A standard. Nothing above is a standard or a requirement. The initiative page describes NIST's role as producing "voluntary guidelines to inform industry-led standardization for AI agents."
> - Agent control overlays. The COSIS project page lists single-agent and multi-agent use cases, but its updates show no agent overlay draft.
> - An NCCoE project description. The summary of comments says the NCCoE "intends to release a draft project description", and the project hub says "the ultimate deliverable will be an SP-1800 series practice guide". Neither has a date.

## Extractos verbatim — cronología del programa (aiagentthreats.com)

> - 8 y 12 de enero de 2026: call for comments on agent security (el NIST agent RFI). Docket NIST-2025-0035. El aviso advierte que los sistemas de agentes "may be susceptible to hijacking, backdoor attacks, and other exploits". Se recibieron 937 comentarios.
> - 5 de febrero de 2026: NCCoE concept paper, "Accelerating the Adoption of Software and Artificial Intelligence Agent Identity and Authorization".
> - 17 de febrero de 2026: **lanzamiento.** NIST anunció la AI Agent Standards Initiative, con tres pilares.
> - 23 de marzo de 2026: red-teaming. «Across more than 250,000 attack attempts from over 400 participants, at least one successful attack was found against all of the target frontier models.»
> - 18 de mayo de 2026: resumen de respuestas al RFI (report 800-5). «Commenters widely agreed that AI agents present novel security threats and that these security concerns present a barrier to adoption.»
> - 29 de septiembre de 2026: resumen de comentarios del concept paper, «from more than 600 commenters». «Almost everyone expressed the need for agents to have distinct, verifiable non-human identities», pero «there was no consensus on the exact means for doing this».
> - 16 de octubre de 2026: diez prácticas voluntarias de manejo de incidentes con NSA y CISA.
> - 27 de octubre de 2026: los diez design tenets.

## Extractos verbatim — NIST SP 2400-102e2026 (9 de noviembre de 2026)

> NIST's Super Intelligence (SI) initial public draft report, NIST SP 2400-102e2026, "Adversarial Machine Learning: A Taxonomy and Terminology of Attacks and Mitigations," is available for public comment **through December 18, 2026.** It focuses on attacks on Agentic SI Systems and corresponding mitigations. […] After an extensive literature review […] this report identifies current challenges in the life cycle of SI systems and describes corresponding methods for mitigating and managing the consequences of those attacks.

## Extractos verbatim — Montreal AI Ethics Institute (10 de noviembre de 2026)

> This piece argues that, alongside verifying who an agent is, standards should also verify where its information comes from before it acts. […] On October 27, 2026, NIST published an initial set of ten design tenets for agent identity management systems. These principles point in a promising direction by emphasizing human-in-the-loop triggers for sensitive operations and recommending that access decisions rely primarily on deterministic mechanisms rather than unconstrained model judgment. However, these tenets primarily govern what an agent can access and when human approval is required, without explicitly addressing where the agent's internal information originates.
