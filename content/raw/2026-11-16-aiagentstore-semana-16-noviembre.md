Recopilación semanal (con actualizaciones diarias). Capturado el 2026-11-16. Incluye material de días anteriores de la misma semana.

## ARTEX: hunt playbook para un framework de ataque agéntico (16-nov, verbatim)

> **What changed:** ThreatDefence published a full detection and threat‑hunting advisory for ARTEX, an open‑source agentic penetration framework that researchers and defenders tie to real campaigns (incl. Storm‑0940); the piece lists how ARTEX organizes planner/worker agents, the exact request patterns it leaves in logs, and indicators of compromise you can hunt for.
>
> **Why it matters:** ARTEX shows how agentic tooling turns multi‑step intrusion playbooks into reproducible, machine‑speed attacks; defenders and platform owners need behavioral detections (timing, chained requests, source‑map reads, rapid password‑spray bursts) rather than only signature blocks.

## AT&T pone casi 500 flujos agénticos en producción (verbatim)

> **What changed:** AT&T has deployed nearly 500 autonomous AI workflows across core operations and says its platform now handles more than 1 million transactions each month. The company processes about 45 billion AI tokens daily and plans to triple its production workflow count over the next year. AT&T's Business Agent Foundry is redesigning whole processes—not merely adding chatbots to existing steps. Current use cases include customer provisioning, network operations, sales prospecting, knowledge retrieval, and planning actions across legacy systems.
>
> **Why it matters:** ... AT&T also requires security, privacy, and compliance review for customer-facing workflows, with human oversight until accuracy reaches the mid-to-high 90% range within an acceptable risk profile.

## LangChain levanta 300 M$ a una valoración de 3.000 M$ (verbatim)

> **What changed:** LangChain ... raised a $300 million Series C led by CapitalG, with participation from GIC, DFJ Growth, IVP, and Sapphire Ventures. The round values the company at $3 billion, more than twice its $1.25 billion valuation from roughly a year earlier. LangChain says its LangSmith platform has 7,000 customers, including Harvey, Rippling, Clay, Nvidia, Toyota, and Salesforce.

## Google lleva un modelo agéntico más rápido a Search y al flujo de desarrollo (verbatim)

> **What changed:** Google released Gemini 4 Hydrogen, a faster model designed for multi-step "agentic" work such as research, data analysis, coding, testing, and codebase navigation. Google says Hydrogen completes tasks 2.5 times faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash, supports four reasoning levels, and is rolling out globally in Search while also coming to the Gemini API, AI Studio, Gemini apps, and the Antigravity development platform.

## AWS: Bedrock AgentCore y Strands (verbatim)

> **What changed:** AWS published a recap of October updates showing Amazon Bedrock AgentCore added a stable AgentCore CLI (v1.0) and an AgentCore Gateway that supports model fallback and connecting existing Step Functions workflows as callable tools for agents. The post also announced Strands Bidi Agents (speech-to-speech) is generally available and Strands Box is in developer preview, plus new cost and billing visibility for Bedrock workloads.

## DeepMind — AlphaProof Code (11-nov, verbatim)

> **What changed:** Google DeepMind published a research blog introducing AlphaProof Code, a multi-LLM, agentic framework that generated over six million lines of machine-checked Lean proofs and formally verified key security properties of Signal's libsignal implementation in about four weeks.
>
> **Why it matters:** ... agentic formal verification as a practical option for deep assurance ... DeepMind open-sourced artifacts and describes a workflow where models do the heavy lifting and humans review one percent of the results.

## Linux Foundation lanza Agent Name Service (ANS) (verbatim)

> **What changed:** The Linux Foundation announced that Agent Name Service (ANS) has launched as a foundation project; ANS uses domain-anchored DNS records and certificate-based signals to register, publish, and verify AI agent identities, with 11 organizations listed as early supporters.
