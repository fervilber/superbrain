# Palo Alto Networks in talks to buy AI agent security startup Clutch for $500M

TechCrunch · 5 de noviembre de 2026 (adelantado por The Information)

## Datos del acuerdo (verbatim)

> If the deal happens, it would be Palo Alto Networks' seventh acquisition this year, according to PitchBook. The cybersecurity giant has a history of buying innovation rather than building it in-house, and it has ramped up dealmaking this year in response to rising AI-driven cyberattacks.
>
> [...] That means the acquisition, if completed, would value Clutch at about 50 times its ARR, a standard way of pricing software companies.
>
> Palo Alto Networks and Clutch Security did not respond to a request for comment. The Information was the first to report the acquisition discussions.

Datos adicionales (MLQ News, citando TechCrunch):

> Clutch was last valued at $85 million when it raised a $20 million Series A in January 2025, led by Lightspeed and SignalFire [...] The company is approaching $10 million in annual recurring revenue, so a $500 million price would be about 50 times ARR.
>
> Rivals are buying in the same niche. Cyera acquired Oasis Security, which also manages AI agents' system access, for $1 billion. Cisco bought Astrix for about $400 million.
>
> Clutch sells what it calls an identity security platform for "everything that isn't a person": service identities, secrets and AI agents. Its tools discover agents running in a customer's environment, set limits on their permissions, tools and credentials, and flag credential theft or misuse.

## Contexto del sector (Globes, Techzine)

> This potential acquisition would further strengthen Palo Alto Networks' presence in the identity security sector, following its acquisition of Israeli cybersecurity firm CyberArk earlier this year for about $25 billion. While CyberArk specializes in managing and protecting identities and access privileges for enterprise systems, **Clutch focuses on non-human identities, specifically the security risks inherent in the use of AI agents.**
>
> But it didn't stop there. In September, Palo Alto acquired Console, a two-year-old startup that uses AI agents to automate routine tasks for IT help desks. That deal was valued at $500 million in stock and cash. Embrace ($325 million), the observability platform Chronosphere ($2.95 billion), and the cybersecurity startup Koi ($231 million) also came under the security provider's ownership. In addition, the company announced the acquisition of Portkey, which also focuses on securing AI agents.

## Producto (NewsCord, resumen de la cobertura)

> Clutch Security develops software to track and secure the credentials used by AI agents, including passwords and authentication codes used to access corporate systems. Clutch defines non-human identities as API keys, secrets, service accounts, tokens, and certificates used in cloud infrastructures, CI/CD pipelines, and AI-based systems.
>
> Clutch's discovery product inventories agents on devices, SaaS applications, self-managed infrastructures, and cloud environments, then links each agent with who implemented it, their tools, their authentication identities, and the resources they can access.
