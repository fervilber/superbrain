# Advancing science & engineering with Antigravity's /teamwork

November 13, 2026 — Vahab Mirrokni, Google Fellow and VP, Google Research.

We share recent results from Antigravity's /teamwork multiagent systems, which enhance human-AI collaborations to solve open problems across mathematics, chemistry, systems software, and ML engineering. All of this work is done in collaboration with human experts, and we are making these tools available externally. Over the past several weeks, teams across Google and academic partner institutions have tackled open problems in math, science, and complex systems engineering that had remained open for years. Using Gemini 4 Argon, Gemini 4 Carbon, Gemini 4 Hydrogen and multi-agent workflows such as Antigravity's /teamwork (building on various Gemini models), these collaborations have produced new innovations across information theory, formal verifications, molecular structure discoveries, and kernel optimization, along with several production infrastructure gains at Google. We are making these agentic tools available in Antigravity's /teamwork (alongside /boost and other agents) so that researchers and developers can apply them to their own work.

First, model generation can be unpredictable, leaving a gap between a single attempt and finding the correct answer. The system bridges this gap by pairing **parallel exploration with dedicated falsifiers** (e.g., the Lean 4 proof checker, unmodified software test suites, execution simulators, web search, or hardware counters) to automatically verify which candidate solutions work. Second, AI models often lose track of details over multi-day tasks because attention disperses across growing context windows. To prevent this, /teamwork **partitions context by construction**: each specialized agent receives only the exact files and tools it needs for a specific step. Coordinators hand off tasks to fresh successors, while critics and auditors challenge and verify results from clean contexts without the original producer's reasoning. Teamwork expresses these workflows as declarative self-improving natural-language patterns and dynamically routes each user prompt to the pattern best suited to the task. These patterns range from distributed coding and iterative problem-solving (packaged standalone as /boost, a sequential solve-and-verify loop refined from prior trajectories) to specialized research workflows like long proof and document review, all of which are available in Antigravity today. This allows teams to design custom specifications from past run transcripts routing across different harness patterns and dynamically improving agentic harness.

Notably, **/teamwork-LEAP fully autonomously produced an end-to-end, machine-checked formalization of the Weil conjectures**, the landmark results on counting solutions of polynomial equations over finite fields that culminated in Pierre Deligne's 1974 proof. This formalization spans about **1.8 million lines of Lean, produced across 28,800 subagent invocations**. The system also synthesized over **919,000 lines of Lean** to produce the first formalization of a recent proof of Hilbert's tenth problem over general rings of integers. It also independently reproduces an end-to-end formalization of a recent proof of finite-time blowup for the 3D Navier–Stokes equations with smooth forcing, part of one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems and open for about 90 years.

External scientists and developers can use Gemini 4 models with /teamwork and /boost in Antigravity and Deep Review today. We have also opened applications giving 10,000 academic researchers six months of free access to our highest usage tier. Upcoming updates will bring our formal proof pattern and additional specialized workflows into Antigravity. These features will be accompanied by deep-dive tutorials on the Antigravity blog in the coming weeks, ensuring that developers and researchers have the resources needed to generate, inspect, and iteratively refine custom multi-agent teams.
